math journey...

It all started a few years ago. Somehow I came across the essay A mathematicians lament and I was deeply move by it. It was like deep yearning that was growing inside me... I had been aware that there was something I didn't know I didn't know about mathematics but I thought that it had to do with knowing how to compute stuff. The essay hinted at beauty and elegance and coherence and playfulness and right there I knew I needed to find that land, to understand and be able to partake in such things in which mathematicians find delight! I started reading every book I could find that purported to show that beauty, stuff like a mathematicians delight, the joy of x, infinite powers, mathematics rebooted, humble pi, Issac Newton (Gleick), and so many others. I read books to learn math like Practical Algebra: A Self-Teaching, Geometry and Trigonometry for Calculus: A Self-Teaching, Quick Calculus: A Self-Teaching (I liked that series), Calculus made easy, Linear Algebra (Hefferon), Mathematics: It's content, methods, and meaning, Tolstov's Fourier series, Tenenbaum's Ordinary Differential Equations, Concrete mathematics... Many of these books were aspirational purchases because even though they were in English it felt like I couldn't understand the words!

### Background 

Growing up I was one of those "good at math". I was home-schooled for a good part of my primary and secondary education which taught me how to learn, kept my curiosity intact (as opposed to how, sadly, often education systems tent to reward "finding the right answer" instead of being curious about why is it the right answer, how did it came about and so on). I learned arithmetic with the Math-U-See manipulative and thus the intuition behind how numbers behave, how multiplication is just a shortcut to addition, how 5x7 is the same as 7x5 and all of that was something I was lucky to acquire very early in life, not just that, it was fun! I could "see" and construct how putting 7 pieces of 5 blocks made 35, but also I didn't wanted to count the blocks every time to I was encouraged to learn the multiplication tables, because it was such a nice shortcut to having to count blocks!

From there I continued to algebra, which felt very natural and playful, and so on with the school grade math. I was unfortunate enough to get a high score on math on my SAT, which allowed me to enter a selective engineering school to study Electrical Engineering, but unfortunately I was assigned to the pre-calc/calc combined class for the "good at math" ones. It was intense! I remember that for the first exam, for which I thought I knew what I was doing, had my TI-89 Titanium ready and such... I got 63%! For the second, hoping to do better given that I thought I knew better from the experience, got a nice 59%!

Due to personal and health reasons I was not able to continue my EE degree but my love for it didn't (and hasn't) abated.
