It’s been a bit since I last wrote anything 🙃. Part of it is just being busy, but a bigger part is the friction of writing a new blog post. As a "proper" engineer, I didn’t want to do things the easy way—I wanted the \*custom\*, \*efficient\* way. So my previous blog was a full \`11ty\` static site: custom scripts for resizing images, a template I found and adapted, everything hosted on bunny.net because I’m too cool for GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages (both free, while I pay a few cents). And it was \*exactly\* what I wanted... until it wasn’t.

From the moment I had an idea to the moment I could actually write, I had to: create a new folder, make an \`.md\` file, add the proper frontmatter, copy screenshots to the right place, and—wait, what was the \`npm\` command to preview the page again? By the time I remembered, the idea was gone.

---

A book I read recently hit me hard: \*Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World\* by David Epstein (not \*that\* Epstein). Without getting too sappy, I choked up because the author was speaking directly to my fears and insecurities. My list of interests is this wild, disparate array of stuff, but I feel like I have to crop it all just to fit on a resume.

---

I digress. The real takeaway? I realized I didn’t want a perfectly efficient static site blog. I wanted a place to ponder and share ideas. I wanted to focus on \*writing\*, not maintaining a blog.

---

This reminds me of when I got into 3D printing. I bought the famous "hacker-friendly" Creality Ender V3 and enjoyed printing stuff at first. But then I got the itch to tinker: I changed the board, flashed custom firmware, added a camera, swapped the nozzle... and completely lost interest in 3D printing. Because here’s the thing: I wasn’t \*3D printing\* anymore. Now there was so much friction just to get a single print done. Was the Raspberry Pi on? Was it connected to Wi-Fi? What was the local DNS? The password? What nozzle was even in the printer? It went from "put an STL on an SD card and print" to "do all this cool, geeky stuff and \*maybe\* get a print."

---

I had confused the goal with the process. And that’s the heart of what I want to say here. As a tinkerer, I love the process—the building, the learning, the problem-solving. But that same tendency means I can easily mix up the things I care about \*for the process\* and the things I care about \*for the results\*.

---

This is especially relevant now with LLMs. They’ve made me realize that even within a single project, there are different types of code with different goals. Some parts of my codebase are just about accomplishing a task—I don’t care \*how\* it works, as long as the results are right. Before AI, this was almost any external dependency. How many people have actually read the React codebase? Most of us just use it and move on. It does the thing, and that’s all that’s needed.

But then there are the parts of the code that are critical, or where I have domain expertise—my core competencies. For those, blindly generating code with an LLM leaves me with discomfort and cognitive debt. There’s no library for that, and I can’t just outsource the thinking.

---

Anyway, this is getting long and rambly... but I think it’s a start.
